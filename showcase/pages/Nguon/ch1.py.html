12h2("1.1. Requirements in the Development Life Cycle")
13p("In the classic life cycle [2], requirement determination is the first activity of the "
14 "analysis phase. Its output "
15 "is the input of everything else: analysts build use cases and class models from it, designers choose an "
16 "architecture that can satisfy it, programmers implement it and testers write test cases against it. An error "
17 "made here is therefore the most expensive kind of error, because it is copied into every later artifact. When "
18 "I wrote the code for Design 01 at the end of September, I could still see decisions that came from the very "
19 "first table of Assignment 01, for example the decision that the shop records a payment status but does not "
20 "execute card transactions itself.")
21p("The course distinguishes three levels that are easy to mix up. A **business need** describes what the "
22 "organization wants to achieve (for example, management needs timely sales information). A **requirement** "
23 "describes a capability or a quality the system must have to meet that need (the system shall generate sales "
24 "reports from current order data). A **design decision** describes how the requirement is met (a reporting "
25 "service reads the order table every night). Requirement determination stops at the second level. Whenever I "
26 "caught myself writing about tables, screens or programming languages in this phase, I moved the sentence to a "
27 "note for the design chapter.")
28p("A second distinction is between functional and non-functional requirements [3]. Functional requirements say what "
29 "the system does: register a customer, validate a cart, record a shipment status. Non-functional requirements "
30 "say how well it must do it: response time, availability, security, maintainability. Both kinds must be "
31 "testable. A requirement that cannot be checked by a test or an inspection is only a wish, and Section 1.14 shows "
32 "three wishes from my first draft and how I rewrote them.")
